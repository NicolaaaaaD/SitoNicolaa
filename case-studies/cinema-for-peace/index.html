Case Study · Cultural Events

Expanding international awareness for a globally significant cultural event.

Cinema for Peace needed to reach global audiences without losing the mission-driven character of the event. Building the right paid media architecture was the first problem to solve.

Client
Cinema for Peace
Location
Berlin, Germany
Services
Google Ads · Meta Ads
Industry
Cultural / Film
All case studies
The Client

A film festival that uses cinema as a platform for peace advocacy.

Cinema for Peace is an internationally recognised film festival and humanitarian organisation based in Berlin. Each year it brings together filmmakers, political leaders, and activists to use storytelling as a vehicle for global change.

The event draws an international audience and receives significant media attention — but digital advertising had not been used systematically to grow that reach beyond existing networks and press coverage.

The Business Problem

Reaching people who cared — before they'd heard of the event.

Cinema for Peace had genuine cultural weight, but awareness was heavily concentrated among existing networks. Growing attendance and engagement required reaching people based on interests and values rather than familiarity with the brand.

The challenge was building paid media campaigns that felt consistent with the event's character — not generic awareness ads, but targeted communications that connected the right message to the right audience segment.

Core problem
An internationally significant cultural event, underseen by the international audiences most likely to care about it. The solution was not more budget — it was better targeting architecture and more precise messaging.

What wasn't working before

Audience reach was organic and network-dependent — growth required editorial coverage rather than measurable paid acquisition
No structured paid media presence across Google or Meta — each campaign cycle started from scratch without accumulated audience data
International expansion was limited by reliance on press outreach, which did not scale reliably across markets
The Insight
Strategic insight
The audience already existed — they were interested in human rights, documentary film, and international politics. The gap was connecting Cinema for Peace to those existing interest clusters through paid channels, at the right moment in the campaign cycle.

Cultural event advertising performs differently from product advertising. The decision to attend or engage is values-driven, not transaction-driven. This meant creative messaging needed to lead with meaning — what the event stood for — rather than features or logistics.

Objectives

Three things this campaign had to do.

01
Build paid reach into international audience segments based on interest signals rather than existing brand familiarity
02
Establish a consistent paid media presence that could be built on across future campaign cycles — not rebuilt from zero each year
03
Ensure all advertising felt appropriate for the event's tone — mission-driven, not promotional
The Approach

How I structured the campaign architecture.

01
Audience architecture
Mapping interest clusters to campaign structure
I identified three core audience segments — documentary and art film audiences, international human rights communities, and politically engaged cultural consumers — and built separate targeting architecture for each across Google and Meta.
02
Messaging by segment
Different angles for different motivations
Each audience segment received messaging calibrated to their primary motivation — film appreciation, activist alignment, or cultural prestige. The same event, communicated through the lens that mattered most to each group.
03
Phased campaign timeline
Awareness before urgency
Campaigns ran in phases: broad awareness first to build familiarity and pixel audiences, followed by retargeting with event-specific messaging as dates approached. This prevented message fatigue while maximising reach during the decision window.
04
Platform selection
Google for intent; Meta for values-based reach
Google Search captured active intent from people searching for the event or related topics. Meta's interest-based targeting reached culturally engaged audiences who might not have been searching but were highly likely to respond to the right creative.
Business Impact

What the campaign delivered.

International reach
Paid campaigns extended the event's visibility into new international markets where organic reach had been limited to press coverage alone.
Audience segmentation
Three distinct audience segments identified and activated across platforms — each receiving messaging calibrated to their specific motivations for attending.
Repeatable infrastructure
Campaign structure and audience data built to be reused across future cycles — reducing the setup cost for each subsequent year's campaign.
Key takeaway
Cultural events don't need generic awareness campaigns. They need precise audience architecture that connects the event's meaning to the specific communities who will find it meaningful.
