Case Study · E-commerce / Market Research

Using demand and trend research to improve product-launch timing and distribution decisions.

Rhode Island Novelty needed to make inventory decisions before committing advertising budget. I provided the demand research that made those decisions more defensible.

Client
Rhode Island Novelty
Location
United States
Services
Market Research · Google Ads
Industry

The Client

A wholesale novelty supplier operating across a large and competitive product catalogue.

Rhode Island Novelty is one of the United States' largest wholesale novelty distributors, supplying a wide range of novelty and seasonal products to retailers across the country. The business operates across a large product catalogue with significant seasonal demand variation.

For a business at this scale, the challenge is not just advertising — it's making better decisions about which products to prioritise, when to ramp inventory, and which markets represent the highest opportunity before committing significant budget.

The Business Problem

Investment decisions were being made without systematic demand data.

At the scale Rhode Island Novelty operates, inventory and advertising decisions carry real financial weight. Committing stock ahead of a peak season based on gut feel or prior-year patterns alone carries meaningful risk when demand signals are available but not being systematically used.

The specific problem: product launch timing and initial distribution decisions were made without a structured analysis of search demand, seasonal patterns, or competitive search share — information that was available in tools like Google Trends and the Google Ads Keyword Planner but not being incorporated into planning.

Core problem
Inventory and distribution decisions with significant financial consequences were being made without a systematic view of actual consumer demand. Demand signal data existed — it just wasn't part of the planning process.

What wasn't working before

Product launch timing was based on prior-year cycles rather than current demand signals — missing opportunities when trends shifted or accelerated
Distribution priority decisions lacked a data layer connecting regional search demand to stocking decisions in specific markets
Advertising budget was allocated to products based on historical sales rather than forward-looking demand signals
The Insight
Strategic insight
Search demand is a leading indicator of purchasing intent. It rises before sales peak and signals emerging trends before they appear in transaction data. For a business making large inventory commitments, search demand analysis is one of the most cost-effective forms of market intelligence available.

For products with seasonal demand, even a one-week improvement in launch timing can mean arriving in market ahead of competitors at peak intent. The research had to answer: when should we launch, where should we focus, and which products show the strongest forward demand?

Objectives

Three questions this research had to answer.

01
When does demand for target product categories peak — and what is the optimal launch timing to capture that demand window?
02
Which geographic markets show the strongest demand signals for specific product categories?
03
Which products within the catalogue show rising trend signals versus declining or flat demand?
The Approach

How I structured the research.

01
Demand mapping
Building a seasonal demand model for key product categories
Using Google Trends, Keyword Planner data, and historical search volume patterns, I built a demand calendar for target product categories — showing when interest typically peaks, how many weeks in advance demand builds, and how sharp or gradual the decline is after peak.
02
Geographic demand analysis
Matching distribution priorities to real demand by region
I segmented search demand by US state and region to identify where demand for specific product categories was highest — and where there was a gap between existing distribution strength and search demand. This shaped both stocking priority and advertising geo-targeting recommendations.
03
Trend classification
Categorising products by demand trajectory
Each product category was assessed for trend direction: rising, stable, or declining. Rising-trend categories received recommendations for increased inventory commitment and earlier launch. Declining-trend categories were flagged for potential reduction in advertising investment relative to prior-year budget.
04
Advertising brief
Research into campaign structure
The research output fed directly into an advertising brief: which products to prioritise, in which markets, at what point in the demand cycle — turning the research into actionable campaign architecture rather than a standalone deliverable.
Business Impact

What the research delivered.

Launch timing
Product category launch timing aligned to actual demand build curves rather than prior-year calendar — ensuring advertising spend began when demand was already building, not arriving late to a peak that had started without visibility.
Geographic targeting
Distribution and advertising geo-targeting decisions anchored to actual regional demand data — rather than assumptions based on prior sales patterns alone.
Portfolio prioritisation
Budget allocation shifted toward rising-trend product categories and away from declining ones — based on forward demand signals rather than backward-looking sales data.
Key takeaway
For businesses making large inventory commitments, demand research is not a marketing activity — it's a planning tool. The advertising work performs better when inventory and launch timing are already correct.
